HUMPBACK CONCLUSION NOTES
based on arcgis heatmap, environmental variables density multifaceted plot, and maxent map

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
**OVERALL**
- Consistent preference for shelf and upper slope environments within ~25km of St. Croix, in moderate-depth water with slightly cooler, fresher surface conditions relative to the available survey background
- Hotspots on the southern transect legs align with the bathymetric slope south of STX, which likely represents a migratory corridor or foraging aggregation zone along the island shelf edge.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

Spatial distribution and bathymetry — 
the detection density map shows humpback hotspots concentrated on the south side of St. Croix and along the southern transect legs, particularly over the shelf slope and in areas of moderate depth. The suitability map confirms this — highest suitability follows the shelf-slope transition zone ringing St. Croix and extends into the Anegada Gap corridor to the north. The density plot shows humpbacks were detected across a wide depth range (glider depth ~0 to -500m) but skewing toward shallower dives relative to background, and bathymetric depth detections peak around -1000 to -2000m — shallower than much of the available survey area — consistent with shelf and upper slope preference.

Temperature and salinity — 
humpback detections track closely with background SST conditions (~27.3°C peak) but show a slightly tighter distribution, suggesting mild thermal selectivity rather than strong preference. Salinity at detection is shifted toward the lower end (~36.7–36.8 PSU surface salinity) compared to background which extends higher, possibly indicating association with fresher, more mixed surface water rather than the saltier open-ocean water mass.

Distance to shore — 
detections peak strongly at shorter distances (~10,000–25,000m) relative to background which is more evenly spread, indicating clear nearshore/shelf preference rather than open ocean use.
